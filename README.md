## Scope of a gutter cleaning visit: every run, every downspout

The scope has two parts, and neither is optional. The first is the gutter run, the trough along the roofline, K-style or half-round, which collects everything the roof sheds: oak leaves, loblolly pine needles, sweetgum seed balls and the grit that washes off shingles. Conway Gutter Pros clears that material by hand, because a hand reaches the packed corner, the low point and the outlet mouth that running water alone leaves behind. The second part is the downspout. Every downspout is flushed once its run is clear, and the flush is the proof. Water leaving the bottom of the pipe freely means the elbow and the bottom run are open. Water that dribbles means the job is not finished.

## Schedule: two to three cleanings a year under Arkansas trees

The schedule is set by the trees, not by the calendar on the wall. A home under oaks, sweetgums and pines needs two to three cleanings a year, with visits in spring and late fall. Spring clears what winter and the first storms of the Arkansas rain season have stacked in the run, and late fall clears the leaf drop. Skipping a season does not save a visit; it hands the next storm a run that is already full. The gutter cleaned in November and then left through winter debris, spring pollen and the first heavy rains is the gutter that overflows in April, which is why the spring visit belongs in the specification as firmly as the fall one.
